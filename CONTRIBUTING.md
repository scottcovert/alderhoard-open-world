# Contributing

Two very different things live here, with very different bars.

---

## 1. Building a game (no permission needed, ever)

Use any open canon you like. Do not ask. Do not credit anyone if you would rather not.
Ship it, sell it, put your own name on it.

If you want it listed, open a pull request adding one row to `games/README.md`:

```
| Your Game | genre | you | link |
```

That is the whole process. A one-line PR is a perfectly good PR.

**The only thing that will get a listing declined** is a game that breaks the rules below
so hard that it makes the universe incoherent for everyone else — chiefly, explaining
things. See "The rules that actually matter."

---

## 2. Adding something new to canon (a higher bar, on purpose)

Canon has to stay small enough that a newcomer can hold it in their head after one read.
Every addition taxes everyone who comes later. So the test is not "is this cool" — it is:

**Does it make the existing canon better without requiring any of it to change?**

Good additions tend to:

- **Explain something already there** rather than adding a new thing to explain.
  (GRISHNAK earns its place by giving the existing `nine` thread a reason, and by putting
  a face on the Turning — neither of which required rewriting a word of the guide.)
- **Work in every genre and every medium.** If it only works in fantasy, it is a good
  idea for your game, not a good addition to canon.
- **Be usable as a rule, not an asset.** Nobody should have to copy your art to use it.
- **Survive never being explained.** If it only pays off when a character describes it,
  it does not fit here.

To propose one, open an issue with:

1. The one-liner. If you cannot get it into one sentence, it is not ready.
2. What existing canon it touches, and why nothing existing has to change.
3. How it translates into three different genres.
4. How a player could notice it without anyone telling them.

Expect proposals to sit for a while, or to be declined without much ceremony. That is not
a judgement on the idea — a shared universe dies of bloat far more often than of famine.

## The rules that actually matter

These come from the world guide, and they are the whole reason this is one universe
instead of a pile of references.

1. **Every game must be fully winnable and self-contained on its own.** Threads add
   flavour, never dependencies. Nobody should have to play another game to finish yours.
2. **Pick two or three threads, not all of them.** Varying which ones is what keeps the
   universe from reading like a checklist.
3. **Genre-translate everything.** A sci-fi game should never literally say "lamplighter."
   Use your own vocabulary and keep the wet-ash coat, the limp, the unlit lantern, the
   question about the sea.
4. **The grey feather and the door that never opens are the easiest to drop into any
   genre.** Favour them.
5. **Never explain the Spindle, the Turning, the Lamplighter, or Grishnak inside a game.**
   No lore-dumps. Whispers only.

Rule 5 is the one people break. It is also the one holding everything up.

## Reserved canon

Six things in this universe are **reserved**: real, canon, and sealed. They are named in
the world guide's RESERVED CANON section precisely so you know they exist and nobody
invents a contradiction by accident — but their design documents are private, and using
one in your game needs per-game permission.

**To use one:** email scott@scovert.com with a one-paragraph pitch. Some are
one-game-ever items, so expect a real answer either way rather than a rubber stamp.

**Do not invent your own explanations for those names**, and do not publish a theory
about them here. If your game wants an unexplained piece of deep structure, invent a new
one — that is more fun anyway, and you can submit it to canon using the process above.

Everything else in `canon/` is yours to use with no permission and no notice.
