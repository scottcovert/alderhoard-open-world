# ELDSEAM

*The open universe ALDERHOARD is set in.*

**A free-to-use shared universe for games. Any genre, any engine, any era.**

This repository is the **world**. It is not the game.

There is no engine here, no cabinet, no level data and no source — on purpose.
What is here is everything you would need to build something that belongs in
this universe, and nothing you would need to lift a finished game out of it.

**Licence: CC0.** Public domain. Take it, change it, contradict it, sell what
you make. You owe nobody anything, credit included.

<h2 align="center">How Do We Benefit From Each Others' Work?<br>Instead Of Being 100,000 Isolated Indie Game Devs?</h2>

- **Many worlds, one universe.** Every game is its own complete world, any era, any planet, any genre. Subtle hints at connections occur across games, in-game games, etc.
- **The connection has no name.** If a character needs a word for it, give them a local one.
- **The thing under it all has no body**, just an arrangement of nine points, so it can turn up anywhere: in space, on the Moon, in a modern town, in prehistoric Australia.
- **It shows up one way at a time.** The nine points are in a landscape, an arrangement, a mystery, an ecosystem, or in somebody's old records ... whatever. They might be a sound, a song, with the ninth one late or off pitch.
- **Eldseam is also what people call their beliefs about it.** Schools, sects and factions, and they argue. Give a place its own school with a plain local name. Just like actual people do in our real world.
- **Places you/we build should feel like something else built here first.** Sometimes for a body that wasn't human: ceilings might be too low, stairs might seem nonsensical, doorways wider than they are tall. Nobody explains them. <ins>So far this is only mostly true about my text adventure games, but it's also coming soon to new games</ins>. I know the text games are not going to be the draw.
- **People came much later** and bolted their stuff onto the old places: cables stapled to ancient walls, warning signs in a language the building is older than. The oldest things are often instruments, still showing their last reading.
- **The five marks** turn up across worlds: a limping traveller with an unlit lantern, one phrase, a grey feather, the number nine, a door that never opens. Use two or three, never all five.
- **Your game has to stand on its own.** Someone who's never heard of any of this should be able to love and finish your game. It doesn't have to be "finished": it can be a public work in progress as long as players can achieve an objective or complete a level.
- **A few names are sealed.** They're listed in the canon; ask before you use one. New places, creatures and factions can join the canon too.

## The whole method in five lines

1. Read the world guide.
2. Pick **two or three** marks. Never all of them.
3. **Genre-translate** them. A hooded pilgrim in a fantasy world is a drifting
   maintenance tech in a sci-fi one. Same coat, same limp, same unlit lantern.
4. Drop them in as atmosphere. Never as plot, never as a dependency.
5. **Never explain any of it.**

Your game must stand completely on its own. The marks are seasoning. A player
who has never heard of any of this should finish your game without noticing
anything was odd — and a player who has should finish it with the back of their
neck prickling.

**Your version is supposed to look different from everyone else's.** A yokai
story, a Nordic-runes story, an abduction in a Nevada trailer park and a
corporate-horror FPS can all be correct and all be the same story, because every
account of this reality is shaped by whoever had the experience. That is not a
loophole, it is the central idea — see [the lens](canon/the-lens.md).
Contradictions between games are two witnesses, not a continuity error, and
nobody is going to ask you to reconcile them.

### With an AI assistant

Paste `canon/world-guide.md` into the assistant of your choice and say:

> Design a game set in the ELDSEAM universe described above.
> Genre: *[yours]*. Follow the rules exactly: weave in two or three marks,
> genre-translated, never explained.

## ⭐ The thing that connects these worlds has no name

All of these places are the same reality, and something connects them. Play one
game and you notice nothing. Play several and the same wanderer, the same
phrase, the same small grey object and the same number start surfacing across
wildly different genres. The doors between these worlds are older than any one
of them.

**That structure is deliberately unnamed.** It had a name in August 2026 and the
name has been withdrawn, because a tower with a beam running through it is
Stephen King's and everything else on the obvious list — thread, spindle, web,
loom, wheel, lattice — is either tired or already tried and rejected here.

⚠ **So do not invent one and publish it as canon.** If your own game needs a
word for it, use your own word and keep it inside your game. That is more
interesting anyway: **every account of this reality is shaped by whoever had the
experience**, so of course the name would differ from witness to witness. The
absence is not a gap waiting to be filled. It is the most honest thing on this
page.
