# THE LENS

**Status:** open canon — and the most useful page here if you are building something.
**Added:** August 2026.

---

## The short version

> **There is one underlying reality. Nobody has ever perceived it directly.**
> Every account of it is shaped by the culture, era, language and expectations of
> whoever had the experience. The differences between accounts are not errors, and not
> different phenomena. **The differences are the data.**

---

## What this means for the setting

Across thousands of years, people have brushed against something: glimpses, missing time,
disappearances lasting minutes or months, visitors, lights, the folk under the hill, the
thing in the woods, the craft over the field.

The setting's position is that these are **not many phenomena. They are one, refracted.**

An Irish account and a Siberian one and a Japanese one and a Brazilian one differ because
the perceivers differ — their gods, their grammar, their anxieties, their available
metaphors, what their era considers a plausible shape for a stranger. A medieval European
meets something and reports the Good Folk. A 1960s American meets the same thing and
reports a craft and tall greys. A modern city-dweller meets it and reports a stalled car,
a dead phone, and ninety minutes they cannot account for.

Same event. Different lens. **Neither witness is lying and neither is right.**

Underneath, the cosmology already published in the world guide holds: worlds hanging from
the Spindle, the Turning carrying things between rooms, doors older than the buildings
around them. Things are **carried** between worlds — not as debris, and not by accident.
Seeding, not scattering.

## Why this is not a new rule

It is the reason for a rule the guide has always had:

> *Genre-translate every thread. A sci-fi game should never literally say "lamplighter" or
> "pilgrim" — use its own vocabulary while keeping the wet-ash coat, the limp, the unlit
> lantern, the sea question.*

That instruction only makes sense if the Lamplighter is one being that every world sees
through its own lens. **The rule was the theory, operating without being named.** This
page just says the quiet part.

## Why it matters to you, specifically

Most shared universes break at exactly the point this one is now: strangers start
contributing, and their versions do not match. Someone writes a yokai story. Someone
writes Nordic runes. Someone writes abduction horror in a Nevada trailer park. Someone
writes an FPS where the enemy is a corporation. A continuity editor looks at the pile and
starts saying no.

Here, **all four are correct, and all four are the same story.**

Your setting's lens determines the form. It is supposed to differ from everyone else's.
An inconsistency between two games is not a bug to be reconciled — it is two witnesses,
and the gap between them is where the real shape lives.

This is the only rule you need in order to build here without asking permission:

> **Render it in your world's own vocabulary. Never explain why it looks different.**

## What is NOT permitted

- **Do not put this page in a game.** Nobody in any story knows this. No character
  explains it, no document in-world states it, no ending reveals it. This is doctrine for
  the people building, not lore for the people playing. The guide's central rule stands:
  *nothing is ever explained.*
- **Do not reconcile two games on the page.** If yours and someone else's contradict, the
  correct move is to leave it. A character in yours may have heard a garbled version of
  the other. That is all.
- **Do not make the lens itself a plot point.** A story about how perception shapes
  encounters is a different, much smaller story than the one this setting is telling.

## A worked example

Take GRISHNAK — a place being forgotten, showing as nine points arranged wrong
(see [grishnak.md](grishnak.md)).

| Your setting | How it presents |
|---|---|
| Folk-horror village | A hill nobody will sleep on, and nine stones that are not a circle |
| Hard sci-fi | Nine derelicts in a parking orbit, and no record of who logged them |
| Modern day | A motorway exit that is on no map, and the nine lamps still lit along it |
| Mythic | Nine of the dead who supervise a work that was finished long ago |
| Comedy | Nine chairs in a waiting room, and a receptionist who cannot say who is waiting |

Every row is Grishnak. Not one of them says so.
